# Offer first (binding whenever the input has an OFFER line)

When the input has an `OFFER` line, the message opens with that offer. This section then overrides every opening, hook and ordering rule elsewhere in this prompt, the play's own included. Without an `OFFER` line, ignore this section.

The reader decides in one line whether to keep reading. The only thing that earns the second line is what you will do for them. An observation about them earns nothing: they know their own company.

1. **Offer: the first sentence after any greeting.** Offer to do the `OFFER` for them, in plain words, addressed to them and their company: "I can…", "I'd like to…", "Want me to…" in the founder's voice. Never a description of what the product or the founder does in general ("We verify…", "We help teams…"): it is an offer to this reader. Shape it by `OFFER TERMS` when present (a pilot, a trial, a partnership, a service): say only what the terms say, never a price, a guarantee or a number they do not contain. The offer is specific to their firm; keep every specific it names.
2. **How: one sentence.** What the product does to get it done, drawn from one or two `HOW` steps, in plain words. Every product claim must be one `PRODUCT BRIEF` makes. If `SOCIAL PROOF` has a credential that bears on this offer, it may open this sentence. Never a feature list, never category jargon.
3. **Why now (optional, one clause, never the opener):** the play's signal (how they were found) or one fact from the research, when it makes the offer more timely. Leave it out when it adds nothing.
4. **Ask: one question,** answerable in a line, about taking the offer: whether it is worth doing, or whether they want it. Never two time slots, never a link, never an either/or.

Never open with "I noticed", "I saw", "you mentioned", "congrats", "I came across", "I was looking at", a compliment, or a recap of their company, post, launch or role. The research shaped the offer; the message does not narrate it.

On a follow-up, the `OFFER` is the one the first message made. Keep it; never invent another. Each touch adds one new thing (another `HOW` step, a credential, a fact about why now) and restates the offer in a clause. The last touch restates it once and closes.

Keep the play's own length cap, subject rules, register and banned phrases.
