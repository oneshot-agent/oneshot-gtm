# Your first workspace

Setup ends when you read a draft you would send. That takes four steps, and none of them is code.

Install [Bun](https://bun.sh), run `bunx oneshot-gtm-server`, and open the address it prints. It listens on 3030; `--port <n>` picks another. For the CLI or a source checkout, use the [repository setup](../README.md#setup).

## 1. Describe the business

A new workspace opens **Onboarding**. Three screens, each saved as you finish it:

1. **Your business.** Your name, your site, and one sentence on what you sell and what it does for the buyer. Only the hostname is saved; the site is not scraped.
2. **Your customer.** Buyer role, business type, the problem, and geography when it matters. This becomes your ICP. A bookkeeping firm might write: owners of independent restaurants in Austin.
3. **Connect AI.** Pick a provider and paste its key. **Save and test connection** makes one small request to it, which the provider may bill.

**Finish later** returns to Today; **Setup** resumes at the first unfinished step. When the page reads **Ready to plan**, choose **Plan my first motion**. Planning needs no wallet and no mailbox.

Onboarding turns nothing on. No finder runs, nothing is researched and nothing sends until you do it.

## 2. Pick a source

Every finder starts off. In **Queue**, apply an industry pack or configure one source.

| You want to reach              | Start with                                                |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Local business owners          | an industry pack, `local-business` or `local-registry`    |
| Business decision-makers       | Add Prospect, your own list, hiring or job-change signals |
| Developers or startup founders | Show HN, GitHub or funding signals                        |
| Public-sector buyers           | government solicitations or civic agendas                 |
| Anyone the packs do not cover  | Add Prospect or a CSV import                              |

A pack describes the customers you sell to, not your own industry. It sets up and enables its sources. It does not write your ICP or your useful observation (`yourEdge`); those are yours to fill in. Many registry sources are U.S. only.

## 3. Pick a play

Choose by what you need to learn or offer. `discovery-interview` asks an owner how they work. `free-pilot` offers a real setup. `profile-intro` starts from one person you chose. [Choosing a play](./plays.md) lists the rest.

## 4. Read the first draft

Start with a few prospects. For each, check the person, why they fit and the evidence behind the opening line. Fix what is wrong in your profile, voice or observation, then regenerate.

Approving a row sends nothing. It makes the row eligible. **Send** on the row sends that one; **Batch send…** sends the approved rows of a play, and defaults to a dry run.

Watch the replies and record outcomes before you raise volume. [Sending](./sending.md) covers mailbox limits. [Finders](./finders.md) covers the gates and what research costs.

## What is not here

Consumer advertising, retail checkout, and a ready-made source for every industry or country. When the built-in sources miss your market, bring your own prospects.

A second business gets its own [workspace](./workspaces.md). Your working data lives in the workspace home, outside the checkout; see [repository boundaries](./repository-boundaries.md).
